01 THE BIG PICTURE

History leaves
more than footprints.

It leaves words in our vocabulary, traditions in our homes, and questions about who we are. During the American colonial period, public education expanded and English became a major language of instruction. These changes opened doors—but also reshaped the place of Filipino languages and cultural practices.

Nag-iiwan ang kasaysayan ng mga salita sa ating bokabularyo, tradisyon sa tahanan, at mga tanong tungkol sa ating pagkatao. Sa panahon ng pananakop ng mga Amerikano, lumawak ang pampublikong edukasyon at naging pangunahing wikang panturo ang Ingles. Nagbukas ito ng mga oportunidad, ngunit binago rin ang puwesto ng mga katutubong wika at kaugalian.

MANILA · EDUCATION

A classroom
of new possibilities

English entered classrooms as a language of instruction. Education offered new opportunities, while raising questions about whose knowledge and language were centered.

Pumasok ang Ingles sa mga silid-aralan bilang wikang panturo. Nagbigay ng bagong oportunidad ang edukasyon, ngunit nagbukas din ito ng tanong kung kaninong kaalaman at wika ang binibigyang-halaga.

CHAPTER 02 ↗

MINDANAO · LANGUAGE

More than
one way to speak

In a region rich with languages and traditions, English could help people connect to wider systems—but it could also make local languages feel less valued.

Sa rehiyong mayaman sa wika at tradisyon, nakatulong ang Ingles upang makaugnay sa mas malawak na sistema. Gayunman, maaari rin nitong mapababa ang pagpapahalaga sa mga lokal na wika.

CHAPTER 03 ↗

VISAYAS · IDENTITY

Tradition in
a changing world

Clothing, music, beliefs, and daily customs changed through cultural contact. Yet communities kept adapting, preserving and reimagining what mattered to them.

Nagbago ang pananamit, musika, paniniwala, at kaugalian dahil sa ugnayang pangkultura. Gayunman, patuloy na umangkop ang mga pamayanan habang pinangangalagaan ang mahahalagang tradisyon.

WORDS THAT TRAVEL

Language can connect us.

English became influential in education, government, and media. Filipino and regional languages, however, remain essential to belonging, memory, humor, and community. Being multilingual can be a bridge—not a reason to rank one language above another.
